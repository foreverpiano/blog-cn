从以编排为主转向以领导为主的管理角色

2025-07-19 · 阅读原文

对于那些长期向某位特定领导汇报，或在目标明确、路径清晰的组织中工作的管理者而言，很容易在不知不觉中形成技能缺口。这通常是因为这些技能在你成长的环境中并不特别重要。你可能会对现有技能极为自信，进入一个需要不同能力组合的新组织，然后迅速摔跟头。

这种情况有几种常见类型，但我在这里想讨论的是：管理者在高度自上而下的规划型组织中成长（“以编排为主的角色”），之后进入足够高阶的职位，或是进入自下而上的组织，这时组织期望他们更多地“领导”而非“编排”（“以领导为主的角色”）。

编排与领导的区别

你可以用多种方式拆解解决问题所需的组成部分，我并非说这是最完美的划分，但以下是指导团队工作的六个重要环节：

  1. 问题发现：找出值得解决的问题
  2. 问题选择：与利益相关者就已发现的问题达成一致
  3. 方案发现：找出解决选中问题的潜在方案
  4. 方案选择：与利益相关者就选择的路径达成一致
  5. 执行：实施选定的方案
  6. 持续修订：在计划演进过程中，保持团队和利益相关者始终对齐

在以编排为主的管理角色中，你可能只关注后半部分的环节。而在以领导为主的管理角色中，你需要覆盖全部六个环节。 那些只在编排型组织中工作过的人，往往根本不知道自己被期待完成所有这些工作。 因此，虽然存在实际执行上的技能差距，但更重要的是意识差距——他们甚至没有意识到这些工作是需要去做的。

以下几种表现，可以用来识别一位仍以编排型思维工作的管理者，他还没有完全理解当前以领导为主的环境：

  • Focuses on prioritization as “solution of first resort.” When you’re not allowed to change the problem or the approach, prioritization becomes one of the best tools you have.
  • Accepts problems and solutions as presented. If a stakeholder asks for something, questions are around priority rather than whether the project makes sense to do at all, or suggestions of alternative approaches. There’s no habit of questioning whether the request makes sense—that’s left to the stakeholder or to more senior functional leadership.
  • Focuses on sprint planning and process. With the problem and approach fixed, protecting your team from interruption and disruption is one of your most valuable tools. Operating strictly to a sprint cadence (changing plans only at the start of each sprint) is a powerful technique.

以上这些在以领导为主的角色中依然有价值，但它们未必是你可以做的最有价值的事情。

在以领导为主的角色中工作

对于进入以领导为主角色的管理者来说，存在一条陡峭的学习曲线，因为这个角色要广阔得多。但必须认识到的是：没有任何高级工程领导职位是只专注于编排的。你要么学会这种领导方式，要么就只能停留在中层岗位（即使在偏向编排的组织中也是如此）。

此外，科技行业普遍认为自己在 2010 年代过度投资了以编排为主的角色。因此，许多公司正在取消这类职位，并防止下一代公司再次创造类似岗位。业界有一种普遍叙事，将这一转变归因于 LLM 带来的生产力提升，但我对此持怀疑态度——这一变化在 LLM 流行之前就已经开始了。

对于正在经历以领导为主角色学习曲线的人，我的建议是：

  1. Think of your job’s core loop as four steps:Identify the problems your team should be working onDecide on a destination that solves those problemsExplain to your team, stakeholders, and executives the path the team will follow to reach that destinationCommunicate both data and narratives that provide evidence you’re walking that path successfullyIf you are not doing these four things, you are not performing your full role, even if people say you do some parts well. Similarly, if you want to get promoted or secure more headcount, those four steps are the path to doing so (I previously discussed this in 如何获得更多人力。).
  2. Ask your team for priorities and problems to solve. Mining for bottom‑up projects is a critical part of your role. If you wait only for top‑down and lateral priorities, you aren’t performing the first step of the core loop.It’s easy to miss this expectation—it’s invisible to you but obvious to everyone else, so they don’t realize it needs to be said. If you’re not sure, ask.
  3. If your leadership chain is running the core loop for your team, it’s because they lack evidence that you can run it yourself. That’s a bad sign. What’s “business as usual” in an orchestration‑heavy role actually signals a struggling manager in a leadership‑heavy role.
  4. Get your projects prioritized by following the core loop. If you have a major problem on your team and wonder why it isn’t getting solved, that’s on you. Leadership‑heavy roles won’t have someone else telling you how to frame your team’s work—unless they think you’re doing a bad job.
  5. Picking the right problems and solutions is your highest‑leverage work. No, this is not only your product manager’s job or your tech lead’s—it is your job. It’s also theirs, but leadership overlaps because getting it right is so valuable.Generalizing a bit, your focus now is effectiveness of your team’s work, not efficiency in implementing it. Moving quickly on the wrong problem has no value.
  6. Understand your domain and technology in detail. You don’t have to write all the software—but you should have written some simple pull requests to verify you can reason about the codebase. You don’t have to author every product requirement or architecture proposal, but you should write one occasionally to prove you understand the work.If you don’t feel capable of that, that’s okay. But you need to urgently write down steps you’ll take to close that gap and share that plan with your team and manager. They currently see you as not meeting expectations and want to know how you’ll start meeting them.If you think that gap cannot be closed or that it’s unreasonable to expect you to close it, you misunderstand your role. Some organizations will allow you to misunderstand your role for a long time, provided you perform parts of it well, but they rarely promote you under those circumstances—and most won’t tolerate it for senior leaders.
  7. Align with your team and cross‑functional stakeholders as much as you align with your executive. If your executive is wrong and you follow them, it is your fault that your team and stakeholders are upset: part of your job is changing your executive’s mind.Yes, it can feel unfair if you’re the type to blame everything on your executive. But it’s still true: expecting your executive to get everything right is a sure way to feel superior without accomplishing much.

现在我分享了自己的观点，我承认自己是故意把话说得有些极端——那些没有意识到这一点的人，往往会强烈质疑其正确性，除非你不给他们任何辩驳空间。其实是存在细微差别的，但如果你认为我整个观点都不成立，我建议你直接和你的经理及团队进行一次坦诚的对话，了解他们对你的真实期望，以及他们觉得你目前做得如何。
