培养项目的参与度

2019-06-26 · 阅读原文

我最喜欢写的文章，往往是那些只对一小部分受众有吸引力，却能精准触动他们核心痛点的文章。到目前为止，项目：掌控不可控之事 就属于这一类。大多数人并不怎么参与带领项目，但真正负责这类工作的人，却往往有强烈的感受和思考。

最近收到了一封特别有价值的邮件，来自一位正在创建新项目的人。他对一个常见的项目难题寻求建议：

I joined my current company as a [resiliency] engineer,
and now my team is working to spread a culture of [resilience]
throughout the rest of the engineering org.

We've identified three teams we need buy-in from for
this program to be successful, but one of them seems
tough to convince.

Do you have any recommendations for getting buy-in
from partner teams?
Is there anything else you've written on this topic?

注意：此邮件内容与韧性（resiliency）无关，已做修改以保护当事人。

项目在最初设计时，通常基于这样一个假设：它们能够通过影响力的方式，而非依靠权威，来推动某种关键的文化转变。

而且，坦白说，我仍然相信项目应该从影响力的角度开始运作！自上而下的权威模式很容易让人跳过创建成功采用所需的基础框架，而采用以影响力驱动的方法，则会迫使你在开始施加压力之前，先完善自己的引导机制。

让我们来看两个不同的项目案例。

第一个项目，公司正面临稳定性问题，你获得了一项自上而下的指令，要求减少故障次数。每支团队都会分配一个故障预算，并且必须将实际故障控制在预算之内。当团队快接近预算上限时，他们开始慌了——他们该怎么办？

第二个项目，你创建了一份可靠性实践清单，推荐各团队遵循。然后你将这份清单集成到部署工具中，这样那些没有遵循某些重要实践的团队，就会在每次部署时收到关于缺失实践的提醒。后来虽然高层发起了可靠性改进倡议，但团队已经清楚该把精力放在哪里：跟着清单走就对了！

思考完这些例子后，对于那些无法从团队那里获得所需项目参与度的人，我有一些具体的建议：

  1. Clearly document the path. It should be extremely clear what steps a team should take to make progress on your initiative. There are many useful forms of documentation: examples of previous successes, tutorials, checklists, documentation, office hours, etc. If folks can’t quickly identify their next steps, they’ll probably not work on it.As an addition caveat, most teams are going to be beginners when it comes to your new initiative. Not only are they beginners, but they’ll likely never be experts, because they have many different projects and programs to satisfy. Design your instructions using your expertise, not on the assumption that every team will develop their own expertise.
  2. Sell the dream. Folks are sometimes skeptical of why something matters, especially when it was something that the company used to not prioritize. This is very common for things like efficiency programs, where companies often prioritize growth over costs early on, and end up with a bit of a growth hangover where folks are convinced the company doesn’t value cost efficiency, even when the company is franatically trying to improve their cost management.The solution here is to (1) create new sound bites from your senior leadership emphasizing the new tradeoff, and (2) talk about it a lot in large, public places that emphasize why this is so important.
  3. Build your dataset. Good programs live and die based on having a very clear dataset that they then build all reporting, analytics and tooling on. This might be tickets or it might be something much more sophisticated, but you’ll struggle to show progress without it. You must do this before using subsequent approaches.
  4. Push information, don’t require polling. When you’re creating a new program, it’s usually because you want folks to prioritize a new area more than they currently are, and in that scenario I’ve found it very important to push information to folks, rather than assuming they’ll come somewhere to review it.
  5. Benchmarking everywhere. Once you have folks engaging, create leaderboards of which teams are engaging the most directly. This creates social proof that other folks are engaging with the program, and makes folks who aren’t engaging realize they’re part of an increasingly small minority.
  6. Celebrate success loudly. When teams do engage, you have to celebrate them and ensure they get credit for their participation. This is a positive feedback loop, where teams that get that credit will then give the credit back to the program in turn.
  7. Add explicit goals. Returning to your leaderboard, the final step is to work with your executive sponsor to add explicit goals around the minimum engagement that teams should be making towards the program. Once you have those goals, instrument them on your dashboards and leaderboards, and make sure that teams are reviewing those goals wherever they review their goals (in quarterly planning or what not).
  8. Escalate with precision. Once you have clear goals that you’ve aligned with your executive sponsor, your goal is then to escalate very specific requests to that sponsor for teams that are having trouble prioritizing your asks. However, you want to make the narrowest possible escalations, to avoid overspending your sponsor’s time and social capital. If you escalate broadly, they’ll usually struggle to help, even though they want to.

最后想说的是，参与度不足通常源于两种情况：（1）团队面临相互冲突的优先级；（2）团队不清楚如何参与。我个人从未遇到过团队是因为恶意、坏意图或其他类似原因而不参与。大家都在尽力而为，如果他们的行为看起来不合理，那是因为你还没有掌握全部信息。
