关于撰写和出版《Primer》的思考

2023-11-03 · 阅读原文

我的第三本书《The Engineering Executive’s Primer》在实质内容上已经完成。只剩最后一章需要通过技术审核，以及一次精细的行文润色，但最艰难的部分基本已经完成。当然，这是作者的视角，流程中还有其他重要工作需要由其他人完成，特别是排版和印刷环节。

每写一本书都是一次学习，我决定与 O’Reilly 合作出版这本书，目的是强迫自己在结构和一致性上更进一步。我认为这两点在《Primer》中都比前两本书有显著提升。前两本书从写作、结构设计到概念编辑都是我独立完成的，虽然也都受益于他人的行文润色。我确信这是我目前最好的“书”，这里我特意用“book”一词，是指它最接近一本书应有的样子——最接近柏拉图式理想中的书。每一章都采用相同的结构，对术语的使用保持一致，不再有因为写烦了就“就这样吧”而留下明显问题的章节。

这对我来说是一个重要的项目。一方面，我相信它能通过帮助提升工程领导力，为2022 年回顾。尽一份微薄之力；另一方面，这是我在2021年初中风之后完成的第一本书。虽然客观上我已经完全康复有一段时间了，但“从不可量化的损伤中恢复”始终是一件主观的事，而创作这本书的过程，也成了一场关于“恢复”的冥想。

从这里开始，我会随意聊聊与 O’Reilly 合作和自出版的对比、如何评估这本书的成功、市场推广的努力，以及接下来打算写什么。

与 O’Reilly 合作

我与 O’Reilly 的合作体验总体非常正面，尤其是与我的编辑 Virginia Wilson 共事的过程。这当然不是给别人的建议，因为每个人的具体情况差异很大，但至少你可以了解我是如何思考的：

  1. In dogs, there’s this concept called “prey drive” which is when a dog’s predisposition to pursue a quarry to the extent that it ignores everything else. Sometimes that’s how I feel when I’m writing, but my target is finishing writing, and rather than running into a glass door, my risk is finishing something quite mediocre and deciding I’m done.Having an engaged concept editor who curbs my worst “it’s good enough as is” decisions was the most valuable part of working with O’Reilly. I also think it’s somewhat hard to self-edit on this, because it’s primarily an energy-level issue, but a recognition issue, e.g. I know the chapter isn’t particularly good, but at that moment I want to be done writing. There’s probably a structural solution to this, e.g. “never make a final decision on a chapter until I feel energized enough to write” but I don’t think I could write at my current pace, while simultaneously being challenged by my full-time work, if I didn’t push the boundaries at times.
  2. Working with O’Reilly really helped me in my progression as a book author versus a blog author. I’m continuing to work on improving my comfort with book-writing as distinct from blog-writing, and their feedback along the way has helped me considerably.Giving one concrete example, I often switch audiences across blog posts (e.g. one is written to an executive, another to a software engineer, a third to an engineering manager), which works in the context of a blog, but does not work in the context of a book
  3. For An Elegant Puzzle and Staff Engineer, I work with Nordlyset to handle publishing rights for non-English editions. This has been a vast improvement over managing the rights myself (which I did once for learning purposes), and I’ve been extremely happy working with Nordlyset. Working with O’Reilly gives me similar support for publishing rights within a single contract, which is quite nice. For me, this is neutral overall, and I will work with Nordlyset again if I publish future books with smaller publishers (or self-publish). However, I was only able to work with Nordlyset because I had already published a book with Stripe Press, if I’d only self-published I imagine I would still be handling these directly
  4. The timeline was very workable, and we came in under schedule. My guess is that I could have gotten the book out earlier if I’d self-published, but it would have been a worse book. I’m already comfortable writing to a schedule, so this was fine for me. I imagine it’s uncomfortable for folks who have not written this way before (e.g. first book and don’t have much experience writing with financial strings attached).At a few moments, I did miss the total timeline control that self-publishing provides. As mentioned earlier, I imagine I would have published a somewhat worse version of this book somewhat earlier if I’d self-published. Generally, I think that’s the wrong tradeoff to make for books on enduring topics (and in my opinion it doesn’t really make much sense to write books on non-enduring topics, when there are shorter formats to use instead), but I did miss the control. Particularly as someone who enjoys writing as a bit of “direct production” as distinct from the extremely team-heavy nature of my day-to-day leadership work. Maybe more importantly, I can imagine a counterfactual sequence of events where I simply couldn’t have published this book if I tried writing it during a busy period at work and didn’t have the option to drop the quality bar to release what was ready whenever my energy ran out on the project
  5. O’Reilly financial terms are meaningfully less generous than self-publishing. My guess is that I’ll make about one-half to one-third what I’d have made self-publishing. It’s true that there are fewer costs for me, but the upfront cost of self-publishing Staff Engineer was about $4,000 USD, which isn’t that much in the context of a book that you expect to sell a few 10,000 copies.For me, I have some indirect financial goals from writing, but not direct financial goals, so this wasn’t a sticking point. In general, I think optimizing for direct financial goals is the wrong choice for most first-time authors, and most repeat authors will have enough data to decide for themselves what the right tradeoff is for them 6. Probably the biggest open question for me is whether publishing with O’Reilly will help with distribution. My guess is that O’Reilly published Primer will sell less than self-published Staff Engineer, but largely due to different pools of potential readers. Lest this sound defeatist, I do think this book will be more impactful on the industry than my previous two books, which is my actual goal 7. I do think there’s some prestige value of publishing with a premier publisher like O’Reilly. Certainly it helps close the question of whether I self-published Staff Engineer (or any future books) voluntarily or if it was because they were simply unworthy of publishing, but that’s not a question I get much or really care about. I learned long ago that small groups on the internet will act as they please, and that it’s not really worth worrying about

评估成功

在思考成功标准时，这里有一些参考数据：我的前两本书到2022年底分别卖出了约4.8万本（《Staff Engineer》）和6万本（《An Elegant Puzzle》）。我预计到2023年底，两本书的销量都会达到约7万本。这不是一个特别精确的数字，因为它基于对《An Elegant Puzzle》过去8个季度线性增长的推断，同时假设《Staff Engineer》在 Amazon 上自2022年底以来的约1.6万本销量，在有声书和其他渠道也能大致保持稳定。我预计《Primer》的销量不会和这两本面向更广泛受众的书一样好，但当初我也没预料到那两本书会卖得这么好，所以最后结果如何，我们就一起等着看吧。

《Primer》的目标读者是任何有志成为工程高管、与工程高管密切合作、或者本身就处于工程领导岗位的人。即使按最窄的定义（“只面向工程高管”），这个群体规模也相当可观。

综合来看，从数字上说，如果《Primer》在第一年（即到2024年底）能卖出1万本，我会觉得不错；如果能卖到1.5万本，我会非常高兴。虽然从情绪上很难完全不受销量数字影响，但在理智上我知道，销量并不是评估一本书的绝佳指标。以销量为导向会促使你去写宽泛、普适的主题，而那些并不是我想要写的主题。我最感兴趣的是去影响那些在真实公司里“做实事”的高管领导者，而真正有价值的内容往往集中在一些相对小众的领域。

这些小众领域通常鲜有作者涉及，往往是因为出版社认为没有足够的市场。这种缺乏竞争的环境让你能够写出既小众、又能卖得不错、同时对行业有实质影响力的作品。《Staff Engineer》就是我个人的一个例子，当时被认为主题太过狭窄而不值得出版，但后来证明了这个市场的存在。希望《Primer》也能起到类似的作用。

至于如何判断《Primer》是否做到了这一点，我有一些粗浅的想法：

  • An early sign that Staff Engineer was impacting industry was when it started getting referenced companies’ job descriptions. This was it directly impacting the vocabulary for Staff-plus roles
  • Another sign was when Staff Engineer began getting referenced by companies in their internal trainings, and in external conference talks
  • The influence became clear when folks in Staff-plus engineering roles started debating nuance and (dis)agreeing with some of the specific ideas. I increasingly believe that driving conversation is at least as interesting as driving consensus, and probably even moreso. Growth comes from encountering different perspectives and evolving our thinking through those encounters
  • Much like I’m easily distracted by sales numbers, I also love references in blog posts and on social media, but I think these are a bit second-order in terms of evaluating impact on the industry. I increasingly believe that only a small fraction of the folks I am trying to reach are active in those spaces, and meaningful signs need to exist beyond that ecosystem

这些指标都没有特别容易量化的方式，很多都需要别人主动告诉我才能察觉。但我想，一年之后，是否产生了这种层面的互动，应该会比较清晰。

市场推广的努力

在写第一本书时，Brianna Wolfson 给了我一些关于图书营销的基础建议：

  1. Books are marketed over the first year they’re published, not just in the first week or month.
  2. Pre-existing distribution channels are much more reliable than anything you can create after publishing, including things like this blog’s mailing list, but in particular social media.
  3. Do three or four podcasts and talks.
  4. Don’t worry too much about doing more.

这个建议至今仍指导着我的做法，同时我自己补充了几点：

  1. Five, continuing to write is my best marketing strategy.
  2. Six, publishing stacks over time: each additional book you publish creates more visibility for all the previous books.

最有趣的优势是会随时间复利的，而我相当确信写书就是如此——只要你始终保持高质量，并且持续为大致相同的受众写作。

下一个项目是什么？

我心里已经有两个比较明确的想法。第一个是聚焦于工程战略（engineering strategy），这是我最近才开始感觉自己有的主题。第二个是《Infrastructure Engineering》，这本书我目前还没完全想清楚该怎么写。我很可能会在这两个项目中选择其一，但还要再过一段时间。目前我的节奏是每两到三年出一本书，所以大概要到2026年左右了！解决工程策略危机
