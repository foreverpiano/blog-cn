入职同级高管

2023-03-27 · 阅读原文

虽然许多公司都建立了详尽的，但新高管的入职流程往往是临时拼凑、混乱无序的。通常虽然存在某种高管入职流程，但由于使用频率太低，始终无法做到优秀。 问题的一部分与类似：每位高管和每个职位都是独特的，很难设计出一个可重复的流程来应对独一无二的入职场景。运行你的工程入职项目成为工程高管

问题的另一部分在于，高管的上级——也就是CEO——通常是因为要解决某个特定问题才招聘这位新高管。 一旦新高管入职，CEO往往就会转身去处理下一个最重要的问题。 这种情况非常普遍，以至于我从高管那里听到的关于帮助同级入职的两大常见抱怨，首先是“这难道不是CEO的工作吗，我为什么要操心？” 紧接着就是“我当初为什么没有更重视这件事？我早就知道全交给CEO是个错误。”

即使CEO亲自参与，大多数新高管也希望给CEO留下深刻印象。你经常会看到新高管一边在入职过程中挣扎，一边向CEO保证自己一切都好。 作为同级高管，你的职责是帮助新高管取得成功，哪怕没有人明确告诉你这是你的工作。

我将同级高管入职拆分为五个主题：

  1. 为什么高管入职如此重要
  2. 高管入职与工程师入职有何不同
  3. 如何分享你的心智框架
  4. 明确彼此的角色分工
  5. 持续投入时间共同工作

同级入职和许多优秀的高管实践一样，实际上很直截了当，只要投入一定的时间和注意力，就很容易做好。 读完这篇文章，你将获得一个清晰的框架，用于帮助新同级高管顺利入职。

这是 O'Reilly 出版的《The Engineering Executive’s Primer》一书中的一章，未经编辑。

为什么这很重要

遗憾的是，一个人可以被视为高绩效高管却实际上没做成多少事，但除非你、你的同级和CEO都在出色地工作，否则你不可能真正成为一名高绩效高管。你可以编造任何叙事，说你的工程团队多么优秀，只是被弱的产品或销售团队拖了后腿，但只有当整个高管团队每一部分都运转良好时，你才可能真正卓越。

打造高效高管团队的最佳工具，是与一位重视团队合作的CEO共事，并在招聘高管时保持审慎。一旦你招聘了某人，剩下的最佳工具就是有效地帮助他们入职。如果没有良好的入职流程，即使能力很强的高管也会犯错，有时甚至是无法挽回的错误。 我见过工程高管入职后，也见过有人大幅增加开支直到危及公司生存，还有人。这些问题预防起来都远比事后补救容易得多。大型迁移飞行楔。

高管入职 vs 工程师入职

作为工程师，你经常需要负责把同级工程师带入团队。 在这种情况下，你通常在资历上隐含比被入职者更资深，但你们都在软件工程这个共同领域拥有专业知识。 而入职同级高管则完全不同。你们是真正的同级，职能之间不存在任何隐含的层级关系， 而且你们往往没有在同一专业领域工作过的共同背景， 因为对方可能是新来的财务、Sales或其他工程以外职能的高管。 此外，工程师（以及大多数职能贡献者）的工作是深而窄，而高管则需要先浅而广地全面了解。

梳理工程师入职与高管入职的区别如下：

  • When onboarding a peer software engineer, your goal is to help them understand your current process and one specific onboarding project well enough to implement that one project. After two or three such projects, the new engineer will be relatively ramped up.The best indicator in engineering onboarding is completing initial projects quickly at a high quality bar, which is typically most dependent on their ability to navigate a large, lightly documented codebase for the first time. Bad signs are making very slow progress on projects, as well as arguing about existing processes before ramping up on them
  • When onboarding a peer executive, your goal is to help them understand the company’s landscape (business, team, project, and process), with a particular focus on the most critical, current issues. There is so much to absorb, that they will get a very broad overview, and then go deep as required by the immediate fires they need to put out.Good executives will immediately focus on the highest priority areas, check their plan with nearby peers before beginning implementation, and bring a positive energy (even if things are an absolute mess). Bad signs are inaction on key issues, changing areas that are already working well before addressing higher priorities, or immediately falling back onto previous companies’ approaches that fit poorly in their new circumstances

分享你的心智框架

George Lakoff 在《别想那只大象》中谈到了为辩论设置框架的力量。 你常常可以用一个简短的短语就建立起某人的心智框架，比如“我们产品正在改变用户的人生。” 这个框架很难被撼动，即使有数据表明用户根本不用你的产品也是如此。 帮助新高管建立对业务准确的心智框架，是你能做的最有力量的事情之一，而这只能发生在这位高管入职的前几周。

如果你要帮助新同级高管入职，我建议在入职前两周就覆盖以下所有话题，哪怕这意味着要取消一些例行会议：

  1. Where can the new executive find real data to inform themselves, rather than relying on existing narratives? The best executives will listen to you, but won’t fully believe anything until they’re able to find data to substantiate your perspective. That’s not because they don’t trust you, but because any seasoned executive has been burned by trusting someone who fervently believed something that ultimately wasn’t true.Examples: Company dashboards. Board reports. Tableau. Looker. Google Analytics
  2. What are the top two to three problems that they should immediately spend time fixing? (This is traditionally the first question to answer, but I think setting the mental frame is even more important!)Examples: CEO is very upset with pace of execution. Friction between Product, Engineering and Legal is preventing forward progress on new initiatives. Personal conflict between two senior leaders has frayed cross-functional relationships
  3. What is your advice to them regarding additional budget and headcount requests? The classic new executive move is to privately request more budget from the CEO, who agrees to keep the brand new executive happy, which often creates a cascade of cross-functional hiring that can significantly impact the company’s cash flow. If you don’t want them to immediately request new headcount, you should have an open discussion with them about the consequences.Examples: Although I suspect the CEO would approve more headcount if you ask today, if you look at Product hiring relative to revenue, we’ve gotten significantly less effective each year over the last three years. I recommend spending time debugging why we’re getting less efficient before further increasing the size of the Product organization
  4. What are areas where many companies struggle, but are currently going well? Specifically you want to steer them away from running their “default playbook” where things are already working.Examples: Product and Engineering are already planning together effectively. CEO is very pleased with the release cadence (perhaps they are instead frustrated that cadence isn’t translating into revenue). Collaborative headcount process is already reaching reasonable cross-functional equilibrium
  5. What is your honest but optimistic read on their new team? Give them a detailed, transparent look into the members of their team, while keeping in mind that any historical gaps in performance may have been connected to the reason the new executive was hired.Examples: Two years ago, Design was an active part of planning product work. Then the Head of Design moved from an embedded model to a centralized studio, and the designers generaly lost context on user and business goals. In particular, the impact I’ve seen on the Design Leads has been quite high, going from some of the highest impact folks I’ve worked with to feeling a bit disengaged
  6. Who do they need to spend time with to understand the current state and the company’s implicit power structure? Especially the longest tenured employees who uniquely hold parts of the business in their heads, and individuals who have significant influence over the executive team that wouldn’t be obvious from the reporting hierarchy.Examples: Head of Quality Assurance or Customer Experience. Long tenured Product Manager. The third founder who isn’t in a managerial role but remains very influential
  7. What is going to surprise them? Particularly things that are done in a unique way at the company, but also quarterly or annual processes whose schedule they might be unaware of.Examples: Business reviews. Quarterly or annual planning. Written documents for every meeting
  8. What are the key company processes, how long have they been in place, and how are they working? How should they propose changes to any of these processes if they feel it’s necessary?Examples: Planning. Budget and headcount. Performance management. 利用文化调研数据

毫无疑问，确实有些同级高管只听得进CEO的指导。 我曾负责带过好几位完全无视我建议的高管，他们一上来就倡导全面重写我们的产品或技术栈。 我为没能更成功地影响他们而感到遗憾，但依然庆幸自己已经尽了最大努力。 虽然很难统计，但我确信，通过在他们行动之前主动分享我的心智框架，我已经防止了很多类似的灾难。

明确彼此的角色

高效的工程组织始于产品、工程和设计之间强大的跨职能关系，而这些关系很大程度上受其高管之间合作质量的影响。许多高管在开始新工作时，都假设这个角色大体上就是自己以前的角色，只不过换了一家公司。这会导致他们对“谁做什么”产生隐含假设，而这些假设可能与公司当前的运作方式不一致，从而引发角色定义上的冲突。 你无法完全消除你与其他高管之间关于各自角色的所有张力， 但你可以通过提前讨论那些有意存在分歧的领域，来避免意外产生的摩擦。

你们应该共同讨论的三个问题是：

  1. 你们各自的角色是什么？双方都应该独立写下自己对对方角色的期待，然后互相分享。如果你发现有分歧，那很好！要么这是一个可以快速解决的意外分歧，要么是一个你们需要花时间共同解决的真实分歧。无论哪种情况，你们都省去了事后才惊讶地发现问题的步骤。
  2. 你们如何处理公开冲突？成为高效高管团队的一部分，意味着即使你们意见不一致，也要对外表现出一致。这取决于拥有清晰的沟通策略。当你们事先就如何处理公开冲突达成一致时，这会变得最容易——通常如果问题不大，就尽量在当前会议中礼貌地解决；但如果分歧太大无法当场解决，就约定带到私下会议中处理，以免把冲突暴露给更广泛的团队。
  3. 当你们意见不一致时，升级流程是什么？你们必然会在某些时候产生分歧，有时甚至是关于非常重要的话题。我当然也曾与同级高管在预算分配、路线图、收购等各种问题上意见不合。大多数高管都是在冲突发生后，磕磕绊绊地形成一种隐性的升级流程来解决彼此之间的矛盾，但你们也可以直接进行一次明确的对话。当人们抱怨公司政治时，往往就是因为对某种隐性升级契约存在微妙分歧：那就直接把它变成显性的好了。

这些工作永远在变化，你们之间的关系才是应对这些变化中唯一不变的因素。 前期投入一些时间虽然要花一点工夫，但长期来看会节省大量时间，并且让你们的关系从一开始就走上正轨。

信任需要时间积累

一旦你们就最初的角色定义和合作方式达成一致，入职的最后一步就是安排持续的共同工作时间。 你们最初的约定实际上不会完全正确，只有通过一起处理棘手问题，你们才能发现其中的问题。高管的日程表很快就排得满满当当，除非你提前预约 recurring 的时间，否则你们只会一起处理那些已经升级到常规日程之外的非常痛苦的问题，而这类话题往往会给早期关系带来压力，而不是帮助建立关系。

我建议安排以下关键事项：

  • Spend some time knowing them as a human. Schedule some lunches or dinners to create a few non-transactional experiences together with this new coworker. Executive roles often pull you towards treating each other transactionally, but it doesn’t have to be that way. Try to make this happen a few times a year
  • Hold a weekly 1:1, structured around a shared 1:1 document, that lasts an hour. Repeated exposure has a magic to it, and an hour is enough time to dig deep into two or three meaningful problems.If a weekly hour feels like too much, it may be a sign that either your functions don’t work together much, or that your company is at a stage where executives are leading in the weeds, which many early stage companies are. Try the weekly hour a few times, but absolutely move to less time if this doesn’t feel productive
  • Identify the meetings where your organizations partner together to resolve prioritization and work through any conflict. These might be weekly metrics reviews, monthly operational reviews, quarterly planning, or something else. Once you identify them, make sure both of you attend at least a few sessions together and afterwards discuss any conflict or disagreement to be better aligned in the future

最后，信任意味着说到做到，而这需要时间来建立。你当然可以立刻就享受和某人一起工作的过程，但只有在对方做出承诺并且你看到他们兑现这些承诺之后，你才能真正信任他们。这件事无法被加速。

能取得多大进展？

如果一位工程师在入职前三个月表现得争辩无益或整体表现特别差，你通常会将其管理出公司。 表现挣扎的高管招聘通常在三个月内就能看出来，但往往需要一年甚至更长时间才会真正离开公司。 那些能力平庸但人很好的高管则会待得更久，经常会无限期地留下来。

作为同级，你唯一能做的就是真诚合作，尽力帮助新高管取得成功。 即使最优秀的高管也需要支持，而通过帮助他们有效入职，你可以显著改变公司的轨迹——以及你自己在其中工作的体验。 即使新高管最终不合适，那也是CEO的工作，不是你的，而过度推动，并不能解决问题。难以共事。
