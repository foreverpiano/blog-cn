在小学担任ALT的教学经历

2008-02-14 · 阅读原文

在我的JET经历中，最具挑战性的部分无疑是小学教学。小学教学的难点与初中或高中完全不同，这也是我认为在高中担任ALT通常体验更好的原因之一（当然，课时少、学生少、工作时间短、学校数量少可能也有关）。

小学教学有几个难点。首先，你与特定班级见面的频率不固定。在神冈，我一个学年只见小学二年级三次，而三到六年级则能见8到10次（一年级见得太少，难以形成规律，可能一年不到三次）。这意味着大多数班级我大概一个月只上一节45分钟的课。

其次，你要面对很多不同的班级。在我的情况下，我大约要教14个不同的班级，每个班有30到50名学生不等。让30到50名水平差异很大的学生都投入到你的课堂中，确实颇具挑战性。学生实在太多了。因此，比较复杂的课程往往难以实施，单纯因为班级人数太多。

抱怨了一阵之后，或许该说说小学课程其实也能上得很好，而且过程相当有趣（至少事后回想是这样）。基于这一点，以下是我教了大约150小时小学课之后积累的全部心得。

  1. Your first day of teaching a particular homeroom you will want to have a short introduction planned, but its important to emphasize the word short. The students do care about you, and are very interested, but they don't understand very much English, and even if your introduction was in Japanese, they are young kids, they don't sit and listen well for long periods of speaking (I mean, most college students have trouble with it, why expect it from eight year olds?).I have developed a underwhelmingly simple formula for calculating the length of your introduction speech. 5 minutes + ( 1 minute * number of interesting props). (Multiple by 1.5 for introductions to junior high school students, multiple by 1.25 for senior high school students).Do not go over the timelimit calculated by this formula. Shorten. Simplify. Add more props.
  2. Also have an extra lesson prepared and by your side. You will inevitably realize while a smiling student walks you to their classroom that they are not in class 3-2 like your schedule says, but are instead in class 5-1, and that you just taught them yesterday.The only tactic for surviving this situation in style is to have extra lessons prepared and on your person at all times when in hostile elementary school environments. This emergency lesson can be something simple. If the kids haven't reviewed in a few months, then sometimes I will break out a huge game of karuta3 and have the kids play karuta with the vocabulary from 3-4 different lessons. Mix that with some other simple activities (board races to add or subtract numbers you say out loud is another easy low-prep backup activity), and you can pack a relatively useful lesson into any classes that ambush you.
  3. Planning elementary lessons is both simple and occasionally overwhelming. In my situation the overwhelming part is the complete absence of any guidelines or curriculum, but in the end that isn't necessarily a bad thing. Here is a pretty basic lesson format that I have been using to fairly decent result:Review.Introduce new vocabulary.Activity using new vocabulary.Review this lesson's vocabulary, or introduce next lesson's vocabulary quickly.Many ALTs like to use songs and singing in their classes, but I, personally, have not. Those who do tend to insert the singing between the Review and Introduce new vocabulary segments.I find that reviewing previous vocabulary for a few minutes each lesson is really essential for retention of the material. Otherwise the students simply don't retain it. Even if your lessons are amazing, if you don't review the material regularly, then the students won't benefit from them4.
  4. Selecting vocabulary and activities is a bit daunting at first, especially when you are doing it completely on your own, but it gets easier after a while. The vocabulary can be almost anything, but its most helpful if you focus on vocabulary you think will help the kids once they hit junior high school.I have done lessons on feelings (hot, cold, happy, sad, thirsty, hungry), sports (soccer, basketball, vollyball, baseball, badminton, swimming), classroom things (pen, pencil, teacher, student, book, desk), body parts (head, shoulder, leg, knee, foot, hand), parts of the face (face, eyes, ears, nose, mouth, eye brow), and less tangible topics like classroom commands ("stand up", "sit down", "be quite", "listen", "wait", "stop", "go"). Keep the vocabulary easy, and limit each lesson to 6-7 new pieces of vocabulary5.
  5. The kids in elementary school cannot read or write English characters. This is your starting rule, and you should deviate from it if and only if you see definite proof to the contrary. For example the majority of my sixth year students can read and write basic letters fairly well (although many of them need a model they can copy from), as can many of the fifth years. Most third years will be hesitant to write even a single letter though.I like to make connect-the-dots worksheets with the alphabet, and they do okay starting with the third year students, but that is with large pieces of the alphabet written up on the board, the teacher walking around singing the alphabet song, and the students helping each other a lot (and might take 10-15 minutes for many students to complete). At the other end, the majority of sixth graders can knock one of them out in three to five minutes, although some students will still struggle to begin.

以上就是我积累的大部分心得。我其实不太喜欢在小学教课，因为我的课程表经常在不知会的情况下变动，老师也不总是来教室，而且很难和那些很少见面的孩子们建立感情。如果你真的想成为一名有效的小学老师，首先必须和班主任建立关系。这意味着你需要主动去找老师，让他们听你的课程计划。

默认情况下，那些名义上由你协助的班主任，在你的课上不会扮演任何角色，除非你主动去找他们并提出要求。这并不一定是因为他们不想参与，只是他们和ALT一样感到困惑，不知道自己该做什么。从ALT的角度来看，这令人沮丧，因为班主任了解学生、知道他们学过什么，而且理论上应该在课程规划和教学中起主导作用，但大多数情况下事情并没有这样发展。

你在小学的角色可能比你愿意接受的更接近“游戏主持人”。我自己对此也不是特别舒服，但老师们的期望就是你能让孩子们高兴起来，给他们一些开心的事情。事实证明，初中老师也有同样的期望，他们会在会议上明确表示，他们并不关心你是否在小学教了孩子们英语，而更关心你能否让孩子们带着对英语的良好态度来上课。

如何应对这些期望和现实，完全取决于你自己。我尝试在五、六年级的课堂中加入一点更学术的色彩，并且觉得效果还不错，但我不确定这是否真的对学生有益。我个人的目标是让那些非常讨厌英语的学生学会读写字母。在小学和初中讨厌英语的孩子，通常是因为他们不会读写字母，并且越来越落后于同学。我不认为通过用英语词汇玩井字游戏就能让这些孩子对英语产生好感。

然而，显然现行体制并不认同我这一点。虽然我五个月后就要离开日本，但我远没有那么确定这个体制是否有一天会消失。
