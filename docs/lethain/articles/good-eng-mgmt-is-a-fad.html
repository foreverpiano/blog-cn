“优秀的工程管理”不过是一股潮流

2025-10-26 · 阅读原文

随着年龄增长，我越来越经常思考自己是否把时间用在了正确的地方，以推动职业发展和人生进步。这也是公司每个绩效周期都会问你的问题：这位工程经理是否把时间花在了有效推进公司或团队发展的地方？

令人困惑的是，根据我的经验，这两个表面相似的问题，答案却几乎没有多少共同之处。本文将花一些篇幅探讨这两个问题，尤其是在我们当下这个特别奇怪的时代——管理者被告知过去十年都在做错误的事情，必须采用一种新的工程管理模式，才能被行业的最新版本所认可。

如果你更喜欢视频版本，这里有一场以相同理念为核心的演讲的练习录音（以及演讲幻灯片）。

优秀的领导力不过是一股潮流

当我在2000年代后期加入Yahoo开始软件职业生涯时，两年间我只和经理有过两次1:1。第一次是在我入职几个月后，他主要问了我关于一位同事工作质量的问题。第二次是在我提出离职、要去 Digg v4 发布：源于迫切的乐观。 的时候。用现代标准评价这位经理会非常苛刻，但他的管理风格与《新机器的灵魂》里的团队领导非常相似：为团队找到重要机会，并扫清组织中可能阻碍目标实现的障碍。在当时的工作环境下，他是一位高效的管理者。

把那种领导风格与2010年代的期望对比，当时吸引、留住和激励工程师被许多组织视为最重要的领导力标准。这在 非常合理，那时预算没有上限，许多公司把招聘优秀工程师视为增长的最大瓶颈。那是一个管理者被明确告知“停止写代码是转型管理的第一步”的时代，而这在当时是好建议！现在回头看，按今天的标准这可能是糟糕的指导，但它让管理者与当时的领导力期望保持了一致。超高速增长时代的生产力

再想想我们当前这个从2022年底开始的时代，高利率终结了零利率政策（ZIRP），而产品化的大型语言模型被认为正在摧毁庞大的工程组织。我们扁平化了工程组织，过去很多专注于协调的角色现在被期望回到键盘前，深入技术细节。于是，前一个时代最优秀的管理者——那些严格按照行业要求做事的人——现在被重新定义为官僚，而不是不可或缺的领导者。

在每次这样的转变中，商业环境都发生了变化，从而产生了对理想领导力的新定义。这非常合理：我们当然希望领导者符合当下的必要模式。奇怪的地方在于，每次转变之后，都会被叠加上一层道德故事：

  • In the 2010s, the morality tale was that it was all about empowering engineers as a fundamental good. Sure, I can get excited for that, but I don’t really believe that narrative: it happened because hiring was competitive.
  • In the 2020s, the morality tale is that bureaucratic middle management have made organizations stale and inefficient. The lack of experts has crippled organizational efficiency. Once again, I can get behind that–there’s truth here–but the much larger drivers aren’t about morality, it’s about ZIRP-ending and optimism about productivity gains from AI tooling.

这里的结论很清楚：随着行业发展，它会对你有不同的要求，而且每次转变都会被包装成某种复杂的道德变革，但本质上几乎总是商业现实发生了改变。如果你把当下任何一个道德故事当作绝对真理，那么当行业在几年后再次转向时，你就会处于非常不利的位置，因为“优秀领导力”不过是一股潮流。

今年夏天早些时候，我还在Y Combinator的CTO峰会上就工程管理演变的这个具体话题做了一次演讲。你也可以在YouTube上观看那次演讲的练习录音，并查看对应的幻灯片。

在领导力潮流中自我成长

如果你接受“当下特别被重视的领导力技能是不断变化的潮流产物”这个观点，那么就会引出一个重要后续问题：应该培养哪些正确技能，才能在今天保持高效，并能在潮流更迭中持续产生影响？

在担任工程经理并与众多工程经理共事一段时间后，我认为存在八项基础的工程管理技能。我个人想把它们分成两组：核心技能——在所有角色（包括入门级管理角色）中都必不可少；以及成长技能——它们的存在（或缺失）决定了你在职业生涯中能走多远。

核心技能包括：

  1. Execution: lead team to deliver expected tangible and intangible work. Fundamentally, management is about getting things done, and you’ll neither get an opportunity to begin managing, nor stay long as a manager, if your teams don’t execute.Examples: ship projects, manage on-call rotation, sprint planning, manage incidents
  2. Team: shape the team and the environment such that they succeed. This is not working for the team, nor is it working for your leadership, it is finding the balance between the two that works for both.Examples: hiring, coaching, performance management, advocate with your management
  3. Ownership: navigate reality to make consistent progress, even when reality is difficult Finding a way to get things done, rather than finding a way that it not getting done is someone else’s fault.Examples: doing hard things, showing up when it’s uncomfortable, being accountable despite systemic issues
  4. Alignment: build shared understanding across leadership, stakeholders, your team, and the problem space. Finding a realistic plan that meets the moment, without surprising or being surprised by those around you.Examples: document and share top problems, and updates during crises

成长技能包括：

  1. Taste: exercise discerning judgment about what “good” looks like—technically, in business terms, and in process/strategy. Taste is a broadchurch, and my experience is that broad taste is an somewhat universal criteria for truly senior roles. In some ways, taste is a prerequisite to Amazon’s Are Right, A Lot.Examples: refine proposed product concept, avoid high-risk rewrite, find usability issues in team’s work
  2. Clarity: your team, stakeholders, and leadership know what you’re doing and why, and agree that it makes sense. In particular, they understand how you are overcoming your biggest problems. So clarity is not, “Struggling with scalability issues” but instead “Sharding the user logins database in a new cluster to reduce load.”Examples: identify levers to progress, create plan to exit a crisis, show progress on implementing that plan
  3. Navigating ambiguity: work from complex problem to opinionated, viable approach. If you’re given an extremely messy, open-ended problem, can you still find a way to make progress? (I’ve 驾驭模糊性.)Examples: launching a new business line, improving developer experience, going from 1 to N cloud regions
  4. Working across timescales: ensure your areas of responsibility make progress across both the short and long term. There are many ways to appear successful by cutting corners today, that end in disaster tomorrow. Success requires understanding, and being accountable for, how different timescales interact.Examples: have an explicit destination, ensure short-term work steers towards it, be long-term rigid and short-term flexible

在花了相当多时间对这些技能进行压力测试后，我相当确信，大多数高效的管理者和管理原型都能被归入这些类别。

对这些技能进行自我评估

对于任何复杂的事情都不存在完美的衡量方式，但以下是一些思考问题，你可以花时间对照它们评估自己在每项技能上的水平：

  1. ExecutionWhen did your team last have friction delivering work? Is that a recurring issue?What’s something hard you shipped that went really, really well?When were you last pulled onto solving a time-sensitive, executive-visible project?
  2. TeamWho was the last strong performer you hired?Have you retained your strongest performers?What strong performers want to join your team?Which peers consider your team highly effective?When did an executive describe your team as exceptional?
  3. OwnershipWhen did you or your team overcome the odds to deliver something important? (Would your stakeholders agree?)What’s the last difficult problem you solved that stayed solved (rather than reoccurring)?When did you last solve the problem first before addressing cross-team gaps?
  4. AlignmentWhen was the last time you were surprised by a stakeholder? What could you do to prevent that reoccuring?How does a new stakeholder understand your prioritization tradeoffs (incl rationale)?When did you last disappoint a stakeholder without damaging your relationship?What stakeholders would join your company because they trust you?
  5. TasteWhat’s a recent decision that is meaningfully better because you were present?If your product counterpart left, what decisions would you struggle to make?Where’s a subtle clarification that significantly changed a design or launch?How have you inflected team’s outcomes by seeing around corners?
  6. ClarityWhat’s a difficult trade-off you recently helped your team make?How could you enable them to make that same trade-off without your direct participation?What’s a recent decision you made that was undone? How?
  7. Navigating ambiguityWhat problem have you worked on that was stuck before assisted, and unstuck afterwards?How did you unstick it?Do senior leaders bring ambiguous problems to you? Why?
  8. Working across timescalesWhat’s a recent trade off you made between short and long-term priorities?How do you inform these tradeoffs across timescales?What long-term goals are you protecting at significant short-term cost?

这些问题大多可以独立成立，但有必要简单解释一下“是否曾被高管拉去处理某类特定项目”的问题。根据我的经验，在大多数公司里，高管会试图把你“挖”到他们最重要的、和你优势匹配的问题上去。因此，如果他们从未尝试拉你过去，要么说明你在那个维度上不被认为特别强，要么你已经忙到让他们觉得不可能把你拉走。

“核心技能”会随时间保持不变吗？

虽然“核心”和“成长”这两组分类对我来说很自然，但在写这篇文章的过程中，我逐渐意识到，有些技能会随着潮流变化而在核心与成长之间切换。今天执行力是基础技能，但在超增长时代它没那么核心，在投资者时代甚至更不核心。

这正是作为工程经理在潮流更迭中取得成功最困难的部分：你需要在所有这些技能上建立足够宽广的基础，否则当时代不可预测地结束时，你极有可能被视为薄弱的管理者。

保持精力，才能保持投入

《The Engineering Executive’s Primer》中“”一章捕捉到了一个我花了太长时间才理解的重要现实：完美的工作分配不是那个在数学意义上最大化影响的理想分配，而是那个数学理想与能够让你长期保持动力的工作之间的平衡。如果你特别热爱写代码，那可能意味着你要比对团队最优的情况多写一点代码。如果你热爱优化组织，那可能意味着你要去改进一个让你个人感到冒犯的、充满阻力的流程，即使它在整体上并没有造成那么大的低效。管理你的优先级和精力。

四十年职业生涯

与保持精力以保持投入的问题类似，还有一点是理解自己在职业生涯的哪个阶段——我在 这篇文章中探讨过这个想法。四十年职业生涯

对于每个角色，你都有机会在不同的维度上做优先级排序，比如节奏、人际、声望、利润或学习。没有“正确答案”，总是存在权衡。你在职业早期做的决策，会在接下来的四十年里不断复利。你也必须在当下生活的约束以及明天可能的生活约束下做决策。在职业早期，我对他人承担的责任很少，有机会在Uber这样公司极其努力地工作。如今，随着家庭责任增加，我不愿意再持续做出那样的权衡，这也实实在在地影响了我如何思考在不同阶段应该优先选择哪些角色。

认清这些权衡，并有意识地做出选择，是塑造职业生涯最具价值的事情之一。最重要的是，如果你不思考这些维度，没有足够健康的自我认知来理解哪些权衡能让你在半辈子时间里保持投入，那你几乎不可能拥有一份真正的事业。
