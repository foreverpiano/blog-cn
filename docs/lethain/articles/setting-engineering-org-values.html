设定工程组织的价值观

2023-01-30 · 阅读原文

Uber 最广为人知的企业价值观大概是 Super Pumped，它不仅曾经是公司的一项价值观，还成了 Mike Isaac 撰写的那本关于 Uber 的书名，以及后续同名电视剧的标题。但对我个人而言，印象最深的一个价值观却是 Let Builders Build。

我曾在 Uber 的基础设施工程团队工作，有一次和一位产品工程经理聊天，他想继续推送一个新功能，而那个功能正在严重冲击生产数据库。我担心加大使用量会导致整个应用出现更广泛的不稳定，觉得他们应该先把查询优化好再继续推广。但那位产品工程经理不同意，他认为我的顾虑违背了 Let Builders Build 这个价值观；要践行 Uber 的价值观，就应该批准加快发布，哪怕这可能会给用户和业务带来重大影响。虽然我个人非常认同 Let Builders Build 背后的理念，但那次讨论也让我深刻意识到，用价值观来运营一家公司有多么困难。

明文列出的价值观本身并不能神奇地完成文化转变，但只要稍加用心，它们就能帮助巩固你已经完成的文化变革。根据我的经验，公司和组织的价值观声明既被过度使用，也被使用不足，深入了解如何融入它们是很有价值的：

  • What kinds of problems do values solve?
  • Should engineering orgs have values at all?
  • When does it make sense to establish values out?
  • What makes values useful?
  • How are engineering values distinct from a technology strategy?
  • How should you roll out values?

最后，我会花一些篇幅，讨论我在所工作过的工程组织里发现的一些行之有效的价值观。

这是 O'Reilly 出版的《The Engineering Executive’s Primer》一书中未经编辑的一章。

价值观能解决什么问题？

明确的价值观能清楚地表明你希望人们如何做决策。如果高层团队成员真正践行并成为这些价值观的榜样，它们就会成为一个鲜活的、有生命力的 artifact。反之，如果宣称的价值观与实际行为不符，它们很快就会被遗忘，变成一个经常被拿来开玩笑的摆设。

基于此，价值观在以下场景中会特别有用：

  • You’re hiring more and more senior engineers and managers, who come with a significant amount of working experience at other companies. Documented values increase cohesion across the new and existing team. They also avoid the scenario where new hires unknowingly practice their previous companies’ values, causing a cultural rift between new and existing teams
  • You’ve made a significant cultural change over the past year to welcome ideas from across the team rather than only accepting top-down ideas, and you want to formalize that change so it persists over time
  • The engineering organization generally does a good job of reusing existing coding patterns and services, but a few vocal engineers are advocating for teams setting their own patterns independently of the organization’s existing ones. Formalizing the reuse of existing approaches when possible will prevent a prolonged conflict
  • You’ve acquired a very small company of five engineers to join your existing organization of five hundred engineers. You’ve been clear in the acquisition process that you’re looking for the new team to merge into the existing organization, and your documented values help them navigate that change successfully

在上述所有场景中，价值观都极为有用，但最重要的是要认识到它们无法做到什么：它们绝对不是启动其所描述的文化变革的有效手段。它们可以庆祝变革、让变革固化下来，但宣称的价值观本身并不是文化魔药。

工程组织应该有自己的价值观吗？

在深入探讨如何制定有用的价值观之前，值得先花点时间讨论工程组织到底是否需要有自己的价值观。特定于组织的价值观往往会引发争议。即使你从未听说过，大多数公司其实都有一套成文的价值观，许多高管团队如果发现你要引入工程价值观，会反应不佳。他们会像钓鱼一样抛出一个诱导性问题：为什么不直接使用现有的公司价值观呢？

最好的情况是，你发现公司价值观可以被扩展以匹配你的目标。你可以拿 Amazon 的领导力原则“Frugality”（节俭）为例，为它加入一些工程特有的细微差别：在追求 Frugality 的过程中，我们会综合考虑构建和维护一个新服务所需的全部成本与支付给供应商的成本，并且我们发现使用供应商通常比自己构建更节俭。对现有价值观进行符合组织实际情况的解读，不仅不会削弱公司价值观，反而会强化它，也更容易得到整个高管团队的认同。

有时候，即使你非常有创意地去扩展现有价值观，还是找不到完全契合的。这时有三条路可走：新增一条公司级价值观，引入工程组织价值观，或者引入工程领导力价值观（适用于工程经理、Staff-plus 工程师，或者最好两者都适用）。

哪条路最合适，很大程度上取决于公司规模、高管团队，以及你希望加入的价值观的类型。有些价值观在工程之外并没有那么相关：比如关于“build vs buy”的价值观，对于那些没有“build”选项的组织来说意义不大，因此更适合作为一项工程价值观。而另一些价值观则可能适用于整个公司，比如 。创造产能，而非争夺产能。

如果这条价值观适用于整个公司，而且高管团队也有热情再增加一条价值观，那么我建议从公司层面入手。维护价值观比创造价值观要耗费多得多的精力，把它加入公司价值观能让你和整个高管团队共同分担维护工作。如果它不那么普适，那么你可以在工程组织价值观和工程领导力价值观之间做出选择。

由于人们常常在内心里强烈相信一家公司应该拥有一种统一的 culture，你可能会发现引入组织特定的价值观往往会遇到意想不到的阻力。如果你的公司已经有一些组织拥有自己的特定价值观，那么自然可以沿用这个模式；否则我建议引入工程领导力价值观，而不是工程组织价值观。工程领导力价值观既能达成目标，又不会与“公司应该只有一套统一价值观”这种根深蒂固的观念发生冲突。

名义上，工程领导力价值观适用于 Staff-plus 工程师和工程经理。但实际上，几乎每个人都渴望成为领导者，并会以团队中的领导者为榜样塑造自己的行为，因此领导力价值观往往会在不知不觉中成为组织价值观，同时又避开了明确提出组织价值观时容易触发的雷区。

除了我个人在领导力价值观上的成功实践，业界也有其他例子，其中最著名的是 Amazon 的 Principal Engineering Community Tenets。（值得注意的是，这些“Principal Engineering”原则其实对任何工程人员都非常适用，无论他们的职级如何。）

什么是有用的价值观？

公司确实宣称了很多价值观，但我在这里要说的是，其中有相当一部分其实并没有用。在制定价值观的过程中，常常有人沉迷于公司“应该成为什么样子”的幻想，而不是它“现在是什么样子”，于是价值观就脱离了现实。这就把一个原本有用的项目变成了至少是无用、甚至是 actively misleading 的东西。为了避免自己制定出无用的价值观，下面我将阐述我对“有用价值观”的简短定义。

一个有用的价值观必须是可逆的、可应用的，并且是诚实的：

  1. Reversible: it can be rewritten to have a different or opposite perspective without being nonsensical.Reversibility is a precondition to the next two values, applicability and honest, but it’s also meaningful in its own right. Effective values guide behavior, and it’s only practical to guide behavior when there are multiple, viable approaches. Irreversible values invite lip-service agreement rather than active participation.Example: Uber’s value of Make magic emphasized a willingness to delay product releases that were merely serviceable until they had a spark of delight. Many companies practice the reverse value of, Ship early and often. Any given successful company might take either approach.Counterexample: Amazon’s Are Right, A Lot. How do you reverse that into a reasonable value? Something like “Are right when it matters” initially sounds like the reverse, but on deeper inspection probably means roughly the same thing. A more extreme reverse like “Are Wrong, A Lot” wouldn’t make much sense unless you extend the value significantly to something like, Quickly Learn from Mistakes, which again, is conceptually very similar to Are Right, A Lot
  2. Applicable: it can be used to navigate complex, real scenarios, particularly when making tradeoffs.Many values are written down and forgotten. To keep a value alive and useful, it needs to be used frequently and visibly by the team. Applicability means a value that contributes to planning sessions, performance reviews, and hiring decisions.Example: Stripe’s value of Seek feedback clarifies the expectation that you should be actively seeking feedback on your work rather than working in isolation. If you’ve completed a Tech Spec without gathering input, then you know that at Stripe you’re not actually done: you need to seek feedback before finishing the work.Counterexample: Uber’s Be Yourself. It’s unclear how you’re supposed to apply this value to a practical scenario. Another counterexample is Stripe’s Be meticulous in your craft. It’s a beautiful aspirational value, but it’s sufficiently broad that two reasonable people can’t align on whether or not it applies to any given decision
  3. Honest: it accurately describes real behavior.A touch of aspiration is OK, but useful values should explain how effective employees navigate the organization as it exists today. If it’s too aspirational, values become a trap for your best-intention employees. More cynical employees will ignore them anyway, modeling their behavior on the actions of successful internal role-models rather than your stated values. The only way for your entire team to operate on the same values is to describe behavior honestly. (If you want to change company behavior, change the behavior first, and document it second.)Example: Uber’s value of Meritocracy and Toe-Stepping has been criticized externally, but it was a very accurate reflection of the actual internal culture. You went into a meeting with folks at Uber, and that’s how they acted.Counterexample: Amazon’ Strive to be Earth’s Best Employer. There are many things uniquely good about working at Amazon, but few would argue that their frugality makes them the best employer. The best employer probably wouldn’t use Amazon’s current vesting cliff (often vesting 20% over the first two years, and 80% over the next two years)

作为测试，我们来讨论几个同时满足这三个标准的价值观：

  • Amazon’s Dive Deep asks leaders to engage with the details at hand. If they’re uncertain about the right path forward, dig in, rather than delegate. This is reversible: many successful companies instead delegate decisions down the hierarchy to whoever has the details rather than expecting leaders to drill in themselves. This is applicable: whenever you’re not sure about a given decision, you should dive into the details before moving forward. This is honest: most folks I’ve worked with from Amazon have embodied this mentality, and are willing to go deep into problems
  • Stripe’ Seek feedback asks folks to socialize plans and decisions before finalizing them. This is reversible: other companies would urge moving quickly by avoiding consensus building. As discussed earlier, this is applicable, because you know any major decision needs to be written up and shared. Finally, it’s also honest: this is how people behave at Stripe (when folks complain about Stripe, it’s often that it works this way too much)
  • Uber’s Be an Owner, not a Renter asks folks to make decisions they’re willing to support long-term rather than assume someone else would absorb the long-term consequences. This is reversible: many early companies want to emphasize doing quick work (e.g. YCombinator’s Do Things that Don’t Scale). This is applicable: whenever you’re making a short-term versus long-term tradeoff, you can apply this value. It was also, more or less, honest, although it certainly varied a bit across team (infrastructure teams were incentivized to be very aligned, whereas growth teams were generally incentivized in the other direction)

这些价值观都真正有助于你判断一家公司是否适合自己，也能帮助你在公司里成功运作。如果你选择的价值观具备这些特点，你就能从中收获良多。

有些人会认为好的价值观应该能抵御滥用。例如，我开头讲的那个 Let Builders Build 的故事，可能因为被那位产品工程经理误解而算是一个不好的价值观。我个人并不太担心这一点。自私自利的人总是会用对自己有利的方式去曲解事物，解决办法是对他们的不良行为进行问责，尤其是当他们处于 Staff-plus Engineer 或 Engineering Manager 这样需要成为榜样的角色时。

有两类特别常见的无用（或者说没用的）价值观，频繁出现，值得单独拿出来讨论：身份型价值观（identity values）和优先级型价值观（prioritization values）。身份型价值观包括 Amazon 的 Are Right, A Lot、Uber 的 Champion Mindset 和 Stripe 的 Deliver Outstanding Results。这类价值观可以是可逆的，也可能是诚实的，但很少是可应用的。你有多常在项目快结束时想：“这次我得专注于做对的事，而不是做错的事”？

优先级型价值观有 Uber 的 Celebrate Cities，以及几乎每家初创公司都会有的某种版本的 Make Big Bold Bets。这些价值观往往是诚实的，但在可逆性和可应用性上都比较勉强。从字面意思看，它们只是一个不完整的投资论点——Uber 应该做一些庆祝城市的工作，你的初创公司应该做一些大的押注——但由于缺少关于其他优先事项的足够上下文，它们无法实际应用。更重要的是，如果你深入挖掘任何一个优先级型价值观，通常都会发现它背后隐藏着一个身份型价值观。比如，做大胆的押注，实质上是在表达想要创新和有雄心的愿望。

虽然你应该尽量避免无用的价值观，但只要它们是诚实的，通常也只是不起作用，而不是有害。所以如果你是作为参与者（比如为公司制定价值观）而不是最终决策者（比如为工程部门制定），就不必花太多时间去反对它们。

工程价值观与技术战略有什么区别？

正如 Rumelt 在《Good Strategy, Bad Strategy》中所述，战略由三部分组成：环境、指导原则和具体行动。思考价值观与战略（无论是业务战略、技术战略还是其他）之间关系的最佳方式是：有用的价值观通常可以作为战略的指导原则。并非所有指导原则都是价值观（例如，你如何应对当前的市场机会通常不会成为一个价值观），但大多数价值观都可以成为有效的指导原则。

例如，“Reuse common technologies unless you see 10x improvement”是一个有用的工程价值观，也可以成为你在应对特定环境时所使用的指导原则。虽然价值观通常以一种脱离形成它的具体环境的方式呈现，但你总是可以反向推导出任何一个价值观背后隐含的环境因素。

默认情况下，如果组织的价值观和战略重叠太多，人们往往将其视为失败，但我却认为它们是紧密相连的。组织价值观与战略的重叠不是坏信号，而是细致、注重细节的领导力的体现。

什么时候以及如何推出价值观

不知道哪里有一本 playbook，鼓励新高管一上任就快速发布新的价值观，就像那种紧张地想要展现价值，最终导致 的冲动一样。别这么做。等等。至少等六个月。如果六个月太长，至少也要等到你能判断某个价值观是诚实的还是纯理想主义的时候。你当然可以在小范围内提前测试一些价值观，但如果你确信自己必须在入职后很快推出价值观，我会建议你先想想这是否是正确的工作，或者 。大型迁移怀旧：退回到令人安心的熟悉工作中

专注于诚实的价值观，你在推出时遇到的“被人直接拒绝”的阻力会少一些，但整个推出过程仍然需要投入大量精力。确立价值观应该遵循 ：明确机会、记录可能的选项、尽早让利益相关者参与以建立认同、在最终确定前进行测试以免让人觉得被强迫，然后不断迭代直到它真正有用。宣布价值观很容易，但引入真正会被使用的价值观要难得多。降低风险的方法是让更广泛的团队参与进来，倾听意见，反复迭代几次，让大家感觉这是共同创造的，而不是自上而下强加的。好的流程是演化出来的，而非设计出来的。

一旦完成最初的推出，你要认识到价值观更像是一座花园，而不是一栋建筑。如果它不融入你的日常流程，很可能会被遗忘：

  • integrate them into your hiring process (including letting candidates opt-out if they don’t like them)
  • explicitly talk about them in new-hire onboarding
  • update your career ladder to require value-aligned behavior for promotions
  • highlight culture-aligned accomplishments in one-on-ones, team meetings, and elsewhere

标准流程推出的一个例外是：如果你最终写的是公司价值观，而不是工程组织或工程领导力价值观，那么很可能会有很多平级高管或 CEO 参与进来，而现实是，高管们很难被约束在一个流程里。这在很大程度上是无法避免的，你只能稍微随波逐流一点。（这也是我认为值得考虑工程领导力价值观，而不是去参与整个公司价值观制定的原因之一。）

我发现的一些有用的价值观

因为有用的价值观必须是可逆的、可应用的和诚实的，所以不存在普世的价值观。特别成功的公司会把自己的价值观输出给下一代公司，这让某些价值观看起来像是普世的。Amazon 的 Customer Obsession 就是一个很好的例子：Amazon 之后成立的公司里，有多少把以客户为中心作为一条价值观？几乎都有！而其中真正复制了 Amazon 对客户那种真挚专注的公司有多少？少得惊人。

虽然你不能简单地把一家公司的价值观复制到另一家，但我认为，分享一些我在工作过的工程组织里发现特别有价值的价值观还是有意义的。我并不是说你应该直接采用它们，而是希望你思考它们背后的逻辑，以及它们是否也适用于你的组织：

  • Create capacity (rather than capture it). This value focuses leaders on creating new capacity from outside the company, rather than fighting internally for existing capacity. Creating capacity makes 把同侪当作你的第一团队。 possible, because it aligns incentives across managers who would otherwise be competing with one another for budget. More discussion on this in 创造产能，而非争夺产能。.Reversible? Yes, many organizations take a “fungible headcount” view on shifting teams and individuals, which encourages leaders to capture capacity to complete their goalsApplicable? Yes, highly relevant in any prioritization or headcount planning exercise
  • Default to vendors unless it’s our core competency. You can also write this as: build versus buy decisions should consider the full implementation and maintenance costs. It is often more intellectually interesting to build commodity solutions than to use existing vendor solutions, but the cost of operating, maintaining and extending those solutions is often much higher. Good build versus buy decisions look beyond the initial implementation cost, and don’t prioritize initial fun over long-term maintenance pain.Reversible? Yes, for example, early Uber had a strong Not Invented Here culture, and rarely used external vendorsApplicable? Yes, relevant to any platform or capability build versus buy decision
  • Follow existing patterns unless there’s an order of magnitude improvement. Engineering organizations often get caught in a slow burning but endless conflict about when to introduce new programming languages and tooling. It’s exceptionally valuable to have a clear answer to align folks on making these decisions. More discussion on this in 探索的量级.Reversible? Yes, many companies encourage introducing new technology or have an infinitely high bar to adopting a new technology stackApplicable? Yes, whenever selecting the technology stack for a new project
  • Optimize for the {whole, business unit, team}. Pick one of these options to help the team understand how they should balance decisions between impacting their team and impacting other teams. It’s also a good example of how values and architecture intersect: you can’t really optimize only for your team if you’re in a monolithic service without causing significant problems elsewhere.Reversible? Yes, there are successful organizations that optimize for the team, ones that optimize for the company, and so on. As long as the approach is aligned with the technical architecture, all of these are very viable approachesApplicable? Yes, useful whenever making tradeoffs across team and organization, e.g. selecting the technology stack for a new project
  • Approach conflict with curiosity. One of my foundational beliefs is that most professional conflict between reasonable people is driven by asymmetric information. If you approach conflict with curiosity, you can quickly learn the missing information and generally make the right decision without conflict.Reversible? Yes, although admittedly on the weaker side. A given successful company might focus on resolving conflict via subject-matter expertise, data, user feedback, and so onApplicable? Yes, useful in any scenario with conflict

虽然可逆性和可应用性是普适的，但这些价值观对你的公司或组织来说是否诚实，则需要你自己去判断。

有用，但绝非魔法

书面的价值观是修复某个固定问题的极好方式，但它本身并不是解决方案。好的价值观会记录事情实际上是如何运作的，而不是把你的公司变成完全不同的另一个样子。如果你正在经历组织内的文化危机，那么你首先需要解决那个问题：组织的价值观不是魔法。

如果你最终确定价值观是解决某个问题的正确工具，那么只要它们是可逆的、可应用的且诚实的，它们就会很好地为你服务。
