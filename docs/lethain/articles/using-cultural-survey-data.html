利用文化调研数据

2023-03-13 · 阅读原文

我在 Stripe 工作时，曾重新设计了总监及以上级别工程经理的招聘流程。我的目标是更好地评估那些总是能说对话、表面光鲜的资深领导者。我想挖掘出光鲜外表下的真实信念和行为。其中一个面试环节是让候选人审阅一份直接下属提交的平庸战略提案，并观察他是否能给出有建设性的反馈来改进这份文档。当一位候选人表示他甚至不会提供反馈，因为提案的质量低到他都不屑于讨论时，我就知道这个面试题有效了。我设计的另一个面试环节则聚焦于候选人如何审阅文化调研结果。

几乎所有规模化的公司都会进行某种形式的文化调研。有各种供应商如 CultureAmp 或 Lattice，也有一些公司自己开发内部工具。文化调研会询问在公司工作的体验、跨团队协作、薪酬等方面的问题。结果被汇总后，会为每位经理生成关于其团队的报告，管理层则会收到关于公司整体情况的报告。

与大多数日常业务职责相比，高级领导者通常不会在文化调研结果上花费太多时间，但这些结果是观察他们数据素养以及如何在广阔问题空间中优先分配有限资源的最佳窗口。这一点对你现有的高管团队——甚至包括你自己——和潜在招聘对象同样适用。而且，它也是通过跟进解决团队关切来赢得团队信任的重要机制。

我们将逐一讨论：

  • Reading survey results
  • Taking action on survey data
  • Whether to modify survey questions
  • When to start and how frequently to run

即使你之前从未运行过文化调研，读完本文后，你也会对如何利用这类数据有良好的掌握。

这是 O'Reilly 出版的《The Engineering Executive’s Primer》（工程高管入门）中未经编辑的一章。

阅读结果

我对有效审阅调研数据的快速建议是：

  • spend a couple hours digesting the results
  • focus more on absolute scores than on relative scores: a high score is still high, even if it’s relatively down a bit
  • take the time to identify all issues, rather than getting caught up in the first couple you notice

仅仅遵循这三步就能做得相当不错。但随着我审阅了越来越多的这类调研，并管理过审阅自己团队结果的团队，我进一步细化了细节。我现在采用的方法是：

  1. Before starting, check if you have whole company access or only have access to the engineering report. If it’s the latter, bring it up at the next executive team meeting. If your executive team leads the company, but isn’t trusted to view the whole company’s survey results, something doesn’t make sense there, and it should be fixed.The executive team may find it uncomfortable to see each other’s survey results, but that transparency to one another is part of an executive team gelling together. Further, it’s very helpful to see if, e.g. Engineering and Product in a given business unit are both frustrated, rather than having to reverse engineer that narrative by hand. The broader comments are also helpful, as you may identify areas where other functions are frustrated working with Engineering, which is valuable feedback that’s easily lost without full report access
  2. Start by creating a private document to collect your notes on the survey. For each major theme you identify, take a screen capture of the data that raises the issue, and add a few sentences of commentary. This is your staging ground for analysis, not your final product, so don’t worry about keeping it tidy
  3. Get a sense of the size of your populations in the report. This helps to build an intuition around what kinds of results might be statistically significant. Most changes will be significant in an engineering organization of 2,000, but if your technical program manager team is only seven people, then even an extreme change probably isn’t significant.Survey tools are strongly incentivized to produce narratives, even if the narrative is relatively weak. This means that you have to be cautiously skeptical of the analysis, particularly any that lacks statistical significance. That’s not to say that you should ignore anything without statistical significance, but instead use the right sort of data: comments, follow ups, and so on, rather than raw numbers
  4. Skim through the entire report, and start to group insights into three categories: things to celebrate, things to proactively address, and things to acknowledge. The groupings aren’t final, so don’t spend too much time on them. Make sure to capture highlights in addition to concerns: especially when things are going poorly, you’ll never want to present a purely negative update
  5. If you identified areas of investment after your last survey, how did you perform there? If you saw an improvement, highlight the success. If you didn’t, form a hypothesis for why you didn’t have an impact
  6. Focus on your highest and lowest absolute ratings. Have these changed? Did you expect them to change?
  7. Focus on ratings that are changing the fastest. What’s driving the rapid shift?
  8. Identify what stands out when you compare across cohorts. How do different managers perform? How about cohorts of tenure, gender, and location?
  9. Read every single comment, and copy the most relevant ones over to your notes document. What are the subjective pieces of feedback that change how you interpret the data? What are comments you might quote when sharing a summary?
  10. End this analysis process by spending an hour with a peer, e.g. the head of product, to talk through your findings. Do this after they’ve spent time with their own report. Sometimes you’ll identify connected themes across the orgs, but more importantly you’ll often find gaps in your analysis by talking it through.You can, in theory, do this with someone on your team, but you’ll end up either putting them in a messy position discussing their peers or having to avoid the thorniest issues, both of which are bad outcomes

到了这个阶段，你已经对数据有了很好的了解，是时候进入有趣的部分了：思考该如何行动。

根据结果采取行动

你常常会看到团队花大量时间运行调研，花更多时间分析结果，却从未真正用这些分析去做任何事情。这会让团队感到沮丧，并逐渐学会忽略未来的调研。在另一个极端，非常真诚的领导者承诺要解决所有问题。团队一开始会因此爱戴你，但六个月后当你报告进展甚微时，这种爱戴就不会那么强烈了。成功的做法是走中间道路：找出几个你认为能真正取得进展的重要领域，然后真正去完成工作。

你可能不会感到意外，我在这里也形成了标准流程：

  1. If you identify any acutely serious issues, take action immediately. For example, a manager whose team is in open revolt, or an ethical issue is raised
  2. Use your analysis notes to select two to three areas you want to invest into until the next survey is run. For example, increasing representation for Staff-plus engineers in key decision making forums, or increasing the sense of belonging for remote employees
  3. Edit your notes and new investment areas into a document you’re comfortable sharing with the broader organization. I would err on being transparent, but a very optimistic sort of transparency: most things that are relatively down are still absolutely high, a recent trend down is important but we’re still significantly higher than historical averages, and so on
  4. Review this document with your direct reports, likely in your team meeting. Review with your People or Human Resources partner. Review with your peers on the executive team. Review the document with two or three trusted individual contributors within Engineering who will likely be more sensitive to the kinds of issues that are easier to miss as an executive.As you get feedback from reviewers, think through the feedback and incorporate it to your best judgment. Sometimes reviewers manufacture feedback to have something to say, but my reviewers have always found something important that I’ve missed, so I think it’s important to listen carefully to the feedback here, even if it doesn’t initially resonate
  5. For the areas you want to invest in, make sure you have explicit, verifable actions to take. If these are too abstract, then it’s hard to build your team’s trust in you because it will be ambiguous whether you actually followed through on your commitments
  6. Share the document with your organization (via email, chat, or whatnot) and schedule a meeting to discuss the findings and priorities with the entire engineering organization. I often repurpose 打造高效工程组织的会议体系 for this discussion
  7. Follow up on a monthly cadence on progress against your action items. I often include the updates in my 高管内部沟通指南
  8. Make sure to mention these improvements when you introduce the next round of cultural surveying. The team will take the survey much more seriously if they know you took action last time.From a mechanical perspective, this will also help you score higher on questions on the survey related to whether you actually make improvements from last time. Unless you do this, any new hire simply won’t know if you’ve made any improvements

虽然这是一个直截了当的过程，但它能建立团队对你的信任，并打造更强大的团队文化。高管很少会被问责，除非他们主动暴露自己接受问责，而这正是一个让你对自己团队负责的宝贵机会。当你对自己负责时，也会越来越容易让你的团队负责。

需要提出的问题

有些高管团队每次文化调研即将启动时都会争论是否要增加问题。我理解这种根本性的担忧：问题可能表述不当，而且它们永远不会问到当前最受关注的话题。虽然确保问题有良好覆盖很重要，但我通常认为默认问题已经足够好了，不值得去修改。

为了解释原因，可以想想文化调研产生的两种数据：

  1. Ratings on various questions, which are valuable because you can compare across cohorts, and they become even more useful as you can see their trend over time
  2. Free-form responses which provide subjective context

第一类问题改动代价很高，因为每次改动都会抹掉该问题的所有历史上下文。偏离默认问题还意味着你无法将结果与更广泛的行业基准进行比较。我通常不建议修改第一类问题，除非参与调研的人经常对某个特定问题感到困惑。例如，你经常会听到大家混淆“管理团队”（the Management Team）和“你的经理”这两个词，因为公司大部分人不知道高管团队自称“管理团队”。这种困惑已经使结果失效，因此一个能提升清晰度的改动，比保留连续性更有价值。

第二类问题改动代价相对较低，因为不存在要抹掉的历史。但根据我的经验，如果人们有话要说，他们会在任何地方说出来，即使问题和他们的想法并不完全相关。增加更多开放式问题并不会改变这一点，所以只要你有几个开放式问题，我就不会担心要再增加更多。

综上所述，在第一次推出前值得评估这些问题，但反复争论是否要改动这些问题，只是高管层在做无谓的细节讨论（executive bikeshedding）。

启动时机和频率

在小型组织中，如果你和团队每位成员都有真实的关系，运行文化调研可能不会带来太多收益。然而，随着组织规模扩大，这类调研会变得越来越有用。我发现邓巴数（Dunbar’s number），大约 150 人，是一个适合确保开始运行这类调研的节点。

大多数组织一年运行两次，这是一个合理的频率。如果你想用更系统的方法来确定合适频率，可以考虑你愿意投入多少时间来解决被提出的问题。如果你已经在努力处理半年一次调研中提出的问题，那么运行更频繁的调研就没有意义。你只会惹恼那些参与调研的人，他们会抱怨你在解决之前提出的问题之前又跑来做另一个调研。

这可能会令人不适

虽然我经常为自己的文化调研分数感到自豪，但有时我也相当尴尬。我曾被指出没有关注团队的优先事项。我努力提升远程工作体验，却发现它不但没有改善反而退步了。我拿过低于平均分的分数，也拿过很差的分数。花点时间，坦然接受这种不适。这说明你在乎！然后，认真接纳反馈，并用它来激励自己解决眼前的问题。
