让工程策略文档更易读

2024-05-18 · 阅读原文

正如 构建工程策略的步骤 中所讨论的，一份完整的工程策略包含五个组成部分：探索（explore）、诊断（diagnose）、细化（map & model）、策略（policy）和执行（operation）。然而，用这种结构写成的策略文档实际上非常难读。这种顺序对于制定策略来说很高效，但对于那些只想快速阅读并应用策略、并不需要完全理解每个决策背后思路的人来说，却是一个极具挑战性的阅读顺序。

本文内容包括：

  • Why the order for writing a strategy is hard to read
  • How to organize a strategy document for reading
  • How to refactor and merge components for improved readability
  • Additional tips for effective strategy documents

读完本文后，你应该能够拿着一份已写好的策略文档，将其改写成一个更易于他人阅读的版本。

本文是《Crafting Engineering Strategy》中的一章。

为什么写作结构会阻碍阅读

大多数软件工程师在学生时代写学术论文时，就学会了如何组织文档结构。学术论文的核心是提出明确论点并提供证据支持，通常是逐步推进直至得出结论。有些商业咨询公司会专门培训新员工掌握商业写作方法，例如麦肯锡会教授 Barbara Minto 的《金字塔原理》，但这属于例外情况。

学术论文旨在逐步展开论证，而专业写作则有所不同。专业写作通常有以下三种明确目标之一：

  • Refining thinking about a given approach (“how do we select databases for our new products?”) – this is an area where the academic structure can be useful, because it focuses on the thinking behind the proposal rather than the proposal itself
  • Seeking approval from stakeholders or executives (“what database have we selected for our new analytics product?”) – this is an area where the academic structure creates a great deal of confusion, because it focuses on the thinking rather than the specific proposal, but stakeholders view the specific proposal as the primary topic to review
  • Communicating a policy to your organization (“databases allowed for new products”) – helping engineers at your company understand the permitted options for a given problem, and also explaining the rationale behind the decision for the subset who may want to understand or challenge the current policy

第一种情况的理想格式通常与后两种存在冲突，这也是许多策略文档难以从头脑风暴阶段进化到正式策略的常见原因。我发现大多数策略撰写者都不愿意花时间重新组织初稿，因此让我详细说明一下我个人在不进行重构的情况下尝试推进时遇到的挑战：

  • Too long, didn’t read. Thinking-oriented structures leave policy recommendations at the very bottom, but the vast majority of strategy readers are simply trying to understand that policy so they can apply it to their specific problem at hand. Many of those readers, in my experience a majority of them, will simply give up before reading the sections that answer their questions and assume that the document doesn’t provide clear direction because finding that direction took too long.This is very much akin to the core lesson of Steve Krug’s Don’t Make Me Think: users (and readers) don’t understand, they muddle through. Assuming readers will invest significant time to deeply understand your document is an act of hubris.
  • Approval meeting to nowhere. There are roughly three types of approval meetings. The first, you go in and no one has any feedback. Maybe someone gripes that it could have been done asynchronously instead of a meeting, but your document is approved. The second, there are two sets of stakeholders with incompatible goals, and you need a senior decision-maker to mediate between them. This is a very useful meeting, because you generally can’t make progress without that senior decision-maker breaking the tie.The third sort of meeting is when you get derailed early with questions about the research, whether you’d considered another option, and whether this is even relevant. You might think this is because your strategy is wrong, but in my experience it’s usually because you failed to structure the document to present the policy upfront. Stakeholders might disagree with many elements of your thinking but still agree with your ultimate policy, but it’s only useful to dig into your rationale if they actually disagree with the policy itself. Avoid getting stuck debating details when you agree on the overarching approach by presenting the policy first, and only digging into those details when there’s disagreement.
  • Transient alignment. Sometimes you’ll see two distinct strategy documents, with the first covering the full thinking, and the second only including the policy and operations sections. This tends to work quite well initially, but over time existing members of the team depart and new members are hired. At some point, a new member will challenge the thinking behind the strategy as obviously wrong, generally because it’s a different set of policies than they used at the previous employer. If you omit the diagnosis and exploration sections entirely, then they can’t trace through the decision making to understand the reasoning, which will often cause them to leap to simplistic conclusions like the ever popular, “I guess the previous engineers here were just dumb.”

尽管这些问题都很烦人，但解决方案其实很简单：用适合写作的结构来写作，然后将其倒转为适合阅读的结构。

为阅读而倒转结构

重申 构建工程策略的步骤 中的观点：只要你是经过深思熟虑且信息充分的决定，改变用于制定或呈现策略的结构始终是恰当的。

虽然我通常认为“探索、诊断、细化、策略、执行”这一顺序非常适合撰写策略，但它在呈现策略时却几乎总是效果不佳。无论是在寻求评审还是向更大范围的组织推广策略时，我都推荐使用倒转后的结构：

  • Policy: what does the strategy require or allow?
  • Operation: how is the strategy enforced and carried out, how do I get exceptions for the policy?
  • Refine: what were the load-bearing details that informed the strategy?
  • Diagnose: what are the more generalized trends and observations that steered the thinking?
  • Explore: what is the high-level, wide-ranging context that we brought into creating this strategy?

在寻求批准时，你可能主要关注“策略”部分；在向组织推广时，则可能更侧重“执行”部分。在这两种场景下，这些都是最关键的内容，应该放在最前面。极少数策略读者想要完整理解你背后的全部思考，他们只想知道这个策略会如何影响自己正在做的具体决策。

绝大多数策略读者只想要答案，而非理解得出答案的思考过程，而这些人往往也是最没有阅读动力的读者。那些真正想深入理解思考过程的人，即使文档结构不是最优，也会愿意投入时间阅读。而那些只想要答案的人，则常常会中途放弃，转而自己编造一个答案，而不是坚持读完文档中实际上已经给出答案的部分。

放大来看，这是一个典型的“缺乏用户同理心”问题。文档作者深陷于细节之中，无法站在读者的角度去思考：如果一个人只是想快速进来找到答案然后离开，这份文档会有多令人望而却步。这种缺乏同理心也导致大多数策略作者拒绝为占大多数的“找答案者”而优化文档结构，而是优先考虑极少数的策略作者。但只要你多尝试几次，就会发现这样做帮助很大。更快的验证方法是，去阅读一份你不熟悉的他人的策略文档，你会迅速体会到，如果对方遵循学术论文式的结构，要找到真正的建议会有多困难。

策略重构

倒转结构是优化文档可读性的第一步，但你不必止步于此。你常常会发现，即使采用了倒转后的策略结构，对于特定文档来说读起来仍然有些困惑。我把这个过程称为“策略重构”（strategy refactoring）。

例如，如何采用大语言模型（LLMs）？ 对倒转格式进行了两处重构。首先，它将“细化”部分合并到“诊断”中，这样能让地图和模型更贴近所探讨的具体主题。其次，它完全去掉了“执行”部分，将相关细节整合到对应的“策略”部分中。

策略重构的本质是在结构妨碍可用性时果断舍弃它。策略结构在帮助我们理清决策思路、分离关注点方面非常有效，但大多数读者更希望一次性看到完整的含义和影响。当思考完成后，就应该重构掉那些思考工具——不要因为某个流程的最佳工具，就误以为它也适用于完全不同的另一个流程。

打造高效策略文档的其他建议

除了上述建议之外，我还积累了一些小技巧，能帮助创建更易读的策略文档：

  • Before releasing a document widely, find someone entirely uninvolved with the strategy thus far and have them point out areas that are difficult to understand. Anyone who’s been thinking about the strategy is going to gloss over areas that might be inscrutinable to those who are approaching it with fresh eyes.
  • Every strategy document should be rolled out with an explicit commenting period where you invite discussion, as well as office hours where you are available to explain how to apply the strategy correctly. These steps help with adoption, but even more importantly they help you identify dissenters who disagree with the strategy such that you can follow up to better understand their concerns.

  • Your template should include consistent metadata, particularly when it was created, the current approval status, and where to ask questions. Of these, a clear, durable place to ask questions is the most important, as it slows the rate that these documents rot.
  • After you release your strategy, disable in-document commenting. This isn’t intended to prevent further discussion, but rather to move the discussion outside of the document. Nothing creates the impression of an unapproved, unfinished strategy document faster than a long string of open comments. Open comments also make it difficult to read the strategy document, as often the reader will get distracted from reading the document to read the comments.

总结

读完本章后，你已经知道如何摆脱制定策略时形成的僵硬结构，打造出一份更易读的文档，让他人无论是批准还是应用都更加轻松。除了最初为便于阅读而倒转结构之外，你还学会了如何彻底重构那些在制定阶段必不可少、但会妨碍理解如何应用策略的章节——而“如何应用”恰恰是策略读者最常见的任务。

最重要的是，我希望你读完本章后能认同：花时间重新打磨一份以思考为导向的初稿是值得的，而不是原封不动地发布它。故意拒绝为读者优化文档结构，是导致许多优秀策略最终未能产生预期影响的根本原因。
