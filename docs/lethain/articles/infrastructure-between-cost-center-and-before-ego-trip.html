基础设施：介于成本中心与自我陶醉之间

2017-03-05 · 阅读原文

最近我面试一位工程经理候选人，问她为什么考虑离开当前职位，她说了一件让我深有共鸣的事：她的基础设施工程团队还有大量工作要做，却没有足够的人头来完成。

这就是基础设施作为成本中心的写照。

另一方面，在里，我也见过基础设施团队对优先级感到困惑，同时组建多个目标重叠的竞争团队。更糟糕的是，他们会为同一个问题配备相互竞争的解决方案，却完全不投入人力去解决最紧迫的工作。在这种情况下，关键的生产系统持续崩溃，而周围却堆满了人员充足、目标模糊的“ glamour 项目”。超高速增长时代的生产力

这就是基础设施的自我陶醉。

尽管这两种极端情况的危险都显而易见，但要找到一个务实的思考框架来优先排序基础设施工作，依然极具挑战性。正因为难度够高，这些年来我逐渐整理出一套思考工具，帮助自己对 incoming 请求进行优先级排序，并决定应该主动发起哪些项目。

使用方法是：思考你当前提供的能力、收到的功能请求以及潜在的新能力，对每一项都从这六个维度寻找机会。这些维度是按照大致的优先顺序排列的，当然你的优先级很可能和我并不完全一致。

基础设施为公司和客户提供以下六大主要价值：

  1. Security is more than “locking things down”, it’s streamlining such that the same tasks can be accomplished more security with the same level of effort. (BeyondCorp and Security Keys are nice reads if you’re looking for inspiration!)
  2. Reliability is more than uptime, harvest or yield, but also hardening your interfaces, and ensuring your oncall load is low enough that alerts are actioned instead of ignored.
  3. Usability is making your software easy to use correctly. If it takes twenty steps to provision a new service, how can you make it only take twelve? If creating an S3 bucket requires an approval, can you at least automatically preapprove a few common cases? If people consistently misunderstand your documentation or how to debug your system, how can you make it more intuitive? If engineers have to recite an incantation to deploy safely, how can you add safety bumpers to make doing it the right way easier than doing it the wrong way?
  4. Leverage is making the engineers on and off your team more productive by making your primatives and abstractions more potent (some would argue 无服务器架构：守门机制与利润空间 is such an improvement over service-oriented computing), or by offering entirely new capabilities (for example, offering a task or batch processing system).
  5. Cost is offering your service for less. This can be reducing maintenance overhead to spend more time on features, and it can also be better picking more appropriate AWS or GCP instances. At Stripe, we’ve been driving cost management as an infrastructure feature–sponsored by infrastructure engineering, as opposed to a financial initiative–and it’s been pretty exciting to see what Aditya, Cory and Davin have done to turn this into an enabling feature. (Relatedly, a bewildering post about QoS、成本与配额.)
  6. Latency is both reducing time to reach your servers (through points of presence, an active-active multi-region/datacenter topology, or connection pooling with established connections), and also time within your stack (via better load balancing strategies, request tracing to find latency hot spots, optimizing database queries).

这当然不是一个完整的框架，但我发现它在规划会议中非常实用。特别是，它能帮助我避免基础设施工程团队常犯的一些错误：

  1. Absconding responsibility for security to a much smaller Security team, when they themselves are building most of the platforms that need to be secure.
  2. Assuming that usability of infrastructure isn’t important because their users are engineers. This isn’t true: even engineers obey the laws of distraction, typos and time. Worse, it’s more common for internal users to churn off a platform because of usability than because of legitimate feature gaps (how many times have you been told that it “actually, is possible” to do X task on Y platform, and then heard a complicated work around?), so lack of investment here tends to cause significant–and in my opinion often mistaken–pressure to build new platforms.
  3. Prioritizing glamorous–and typically complex– projects which happen to solve a problem (a solution seeking a problem), rather than taking the direct path to addressing the problem at hand.
  4. Accepting the current status quo as good enough, and languishing with a decent system instead of continuing to push towards something phenomenal.
  5. Delegating cost awareness to another organization who don’t have insight into how your systems actually work, forcing that organization to try to drive cost reductions without enough context to do it well.
  6. (It turns out there are a lot of frequent mistakes.)

通过在规划时系统性地思考这六大价值，我发现自己更不容易掉进那些诱人的思维陷阱。

过去六个月左右，我一直在使用这个框架的简化版本，总体相当满意。虽然直到写这篇文章时，我才真正把易用性和杠杆效应之间的细微差别梳理清楚，而且毫无疑问我还会一直迭代下去。你是否在使用类似的东西？你又是如何避免掉入基础设施规划中的常见陷阱的呢？
